Beyond the Plateau: Where Taiwan's E-commerce Growth Goes Next

Is Taiwan's online retail really flattening, or just normalizing after the pandemic? If it is, where should a brand seller put channel budget as Coupang and cross-border platforms reshuffle the market?

ViewBrand seller choosing channels and budget
Period2019–2025
SourcesMOEA statistics, company results, customs data · A/B
OutputPlateau test, channel matrix, four moves

The short answer

The plateau is real. Online retail grew 29% a year in 2020–2021 but only 2.7% in 2023–2025, and its share of retail has stayed within 0.9 points (13–14%) since 2021. Growth has not stopped; it has moved. It is going to Coupang, to cross-border imports, to physical retailers selling online, and to on-platform advertising, while the largest domestic marketplace shrank in 2025. For a brand seller, that means defending marketplace share on margin, not on price, and moving new budget into OMO, retail media and selective tests on Coupang.

Test rule (DuckDB): plateau if average growth in 2023–2025 is under 5% and the share moves less than 1.5 points across 2021–2025. Result: plateau. Total retail sales fell 0.2% in 2025 while consumer prices rose 1.7%, so online's 2.8% is close to flat in real terms.

2 · Where the growth went

Domestic leader−3.5%momo revenue fell in 2025, its first annual decline since listing; operating profit fell 22%. PChome was down 4.2% in the first half.
Coupang TaiwanTriple digitsTaiwan revenue grew triple digits year-on-year in Q4 2025. The segment that includes Taiwan reached $4.9B (+38%) but loses about $1B a year: growth bought with subsidies.
Cross-border imports+14.7%Small-parcel imports passed 65 million in 2025, worth about 11% of domestic e-commerce. China and Hong Kong send most low-value duty-free parcels.
Physical retailers online31%Share of all retail online sales made by store-based retailers in 2024, up 7.2 points from 2019. OMO is taking share from pure e-commerce.
Retail media+50%Brands buying momo Ads grew over 50% in 2025, while Taiwan's digital ad market grew 3.9%, its third year near 4%.
Discovery shifts to videoVideo > TVDigital video ad spend passed TV in 2025; search ads grew only 3.9%. Social platforms are the main entry point for cross-border buying.

3 · Channel opportunity matrix for a brand seller

Hover or tab to a point

Placement uses the signals in section 2: growth from the reported numbers, readiness from whether a brand can buy, measure and scale the channel today. Quick commerce sits in Watch only because no grade A or B Taiwan data was found.

4 · Four moves

01

Defend marketplaces on margin, not price

Generalist platforms are flat and cross-border sellers win on price for standardized goods. Keep share with bundles, exclusives and service; stop chasing price matches that only shrink margin.

02

Move new budget to OMO and members

Store-based retailers keep gaining share of online sales. Click-and-collect, member pricing and store-to-online offers turn a flat market into repeat purchases.

03

Buy retail media where the purchase happens

On-platform ads are growing far faster than the ad market and are measured against sales. Shift part of search and social spend there, tracking ROAS per SKU.

04

Test Coupang with a limited SKU set

Fast growth, but subsidy-funded and price-led. Enter with products that hold margin at Coupang prices, and set an exit rule before scaling.

Limits, stated plainly

  • MOEA's retail online sales cover registered retailers; cross-border purchases are counted separately in customs data, so the two are not added together.
  • Coupang reports Taiwan inside a wider segment; only the growth rate for Taiwan is disclosed, not its revenue.
  • Shopee does not disclose Taiwan revenue, so it is not sized here.
  • Several MOEA figures are taken from news reports quoting the official release; the 2024 value is derived from the 2025 total and its growth rate.
