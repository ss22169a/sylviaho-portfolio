01The problem

1–2 hrsDaily time on reports and ad tweaks before the workflow (estimate)
900+Part numbers to price-check

Reading reports and adjusting ads took real time every day, and longer when results were poor. With so many part numbers, competitor prices could not be checked one by one, so loss-making products were often spotted late.

02Why AI

Measurable anomalies, such as a spike in spend, get a fixed-rule alert; work that needs judgment, such as matching the same part number or classifying search intent, goes to AI, because it has too many exceptions and too much volume for rules or manual work. After search intent is classified, it is used to exclude weak keywords and add ads for the most-searched models.

03The data

Ad and Amazon back-end data now flow into one sheet automatically, with no daily manual export. The data has its own issues: orders arrive late and rows sometimes repeat, so every row records its source and date, so anything doubtful can be traced back.

04How I judge whether it works

roughly 100–250% higherOne product line vs. the same part from competitors; its ads were paused

Two layers:

  • Is the AI right? I spot-check a sample, comparing competitor prices and specs.
  • Is the business better? I look at ad traffic and product-line profit.

05High-risk actions stay with people

High-risk actions such as editing product page content and allocating ad budget still go through human review; AI does not write changes on its own. Price checks and anomaly monitoring need to be stable first, then AI-assisted suggestions are brought in step by step.

06Risks and checks

±20%Cap on any single adjustment; beyond that needs my approval

Any change involving budget spend, product listings or system settings is carried out only after I approve it. In practice I have seen the AI misclassify items, and source data errors lead to wrong conclusions, so my review covers both the source data going in and the AI's output.

07Rule iteration and refinement

AI misjudgments often trace back to poor underlying data quality. So I am setting up regular spot checks and an error log, turning frequent misjudgments into check rules written back into the workflow. Once stability meets the bar, budget adjustment suggestions will be opened up step by step.
