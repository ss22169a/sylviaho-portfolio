AI-Assisted Pricing and Ad Monitoring for E-commerce

AI gathers the data and flags what looks wrong, so I can focus on the decisions that need judgment.

01The problem

Reading reports and adjusting ads took real time every day, and longer when results were poor. With so many part numbers, competitor prices could not be checked one by one, so loss-making products were often spotted late.

02Why AI

Things with a clear threshold, such as a sudden spike in spend, only need a fixed rule and an alert. Things that need judgment, such as whether two listings are the same part or what buying intent a search term shows, have too many exceptions for rules and too much volume for manual work, so they go to AI.

03The data

Ad and Amazon back-end data now flow into one sheet automatically, with no daily manual export. The data has its own issues: orders arrive late and rows sometimes repeat, so every row records its source and date, so anything doubtful can be traced back.

04How I judge whether it works

Two layers:

  • Is the AI right? I spot-check a sample, comparing competitor prices and specs.
  • Is the business better? I look at ad traffic and product-line profit.

05What AI is deliberately not allowed to do

AI cannot edit product pages and cannot set budgets yet. It has to make monitoring and price checks reliable first; more is opened up step by step.

06Risks and checks

Spending, listing and changing settings only happen after I approve them. I have seen the AI misclassify items, and draw conclusions from source data that was itself filed wrong, so I check not only the AI's answers but also the data it reads.

07What I learned

Many AI errors come from problems in the data itself. Next, I will spot-check on a fixed schedule and log the errors, turn the common mistakes into rules for the AI, and once that is stable, let it help draft budget suggestions.
