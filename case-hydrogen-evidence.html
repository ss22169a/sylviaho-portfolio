Go-to-Market Strategy for a MEMS Hydrogen Sensor

Using data to narrow down the questions, turning market intelligence into precise launch decisions.

RoleProject Manager
PeriodNov 2026 – present
ToolsGoogle Sheets · datasheets · IEA & industry data · Claude
StatusThird-party validation in progress

Conclusion

Market research estimates differ widely in method and reliability, so public financial filings and regulations became the demand baseline instead: the steady momentum is in hydrogen facilities and alarm regulations, fuel-cell cars are small and volatile, and the product's response speed is on par with the industry. The launch case starts with facility demand and differences that can be verified, with third-party validation moving forward in parallel.

Instead of one market-size figure, the demand read comes from what buyers actually paid detection specialists. Growth sits in facilities and new regulation-driven alarms, while industrial fixed detectors were flat. On hydrogen specifically, New Cosmos reports in-vehicle detector samples with fuel-cell bus, truck and forklift makers, and certification of a battery-powered household hydrogen alarm in Mar 2026, but also says Europe's hydrogen market is moving slower than expected.

Why no market-size number: seven commercial reports put hydrogen sensors anywhere from US$20M to US$494M, a 24.2× spread caused by different scopes. All are grade C, so none is quoted. Wider context: the MEMS market is US$17.1B in 2025, heading to US$24B by 2031.

3 · How the decisions took shape

Diverge → filter → converge → result
  1. Diverge

    Set the research scope

    Before collecting a single number, I listed the questions a buyer, a distributor and an investor would each care about most, then let those questions decide what data to look for.

  2. Diverge

    Collect wide, from chip to detector

    I mapped vendors at every layer (sensor chips, modules and explosion-proof detectors) because a hydrogen buyer compares across all three, not just against companies like us.

  3. Filter

    Grade every number before using it

    Each figure got an A–D evidence grade and a check date. Only A and B can leave the company. This rule did more to shape the conclusions than any single source.

  4. Filter

    Cross-check, then audit my own work

    Demand indicators were checked against a second independent source wherever one existed. A full audit on Nov 7 found 27 problems, from a mis-keyed year to a rival filed under the wrong principle.

  5. Converge

    Reduce to conclusions

    Each conclusion is tagged with its sources and the weakest signal grade among them, so every inference can be traced back to the original evidence.

  6. Result

    Turn conclusions into launch decisions

    The analysis ended in decisions the sales and marketing teams can act on directly; items still awaiting test validation went on a separate list, to be settled once results are in.

4 · The decisions

Where to sell firstFacilitiesHydrogen stations, hydrogen production and storage sites, semiconductor fabs and energy storage. Fuel-cell vehicles stay as a showcase, not the volume plan.
How to differentiateProofThermal-conductivity sensing needs no catalyst and no oxygen, so it suits sealed battery rooms and storage sites. The story is built on drift, service life and side-by-side tests.
What to say whenClaim mapNever quote a single market-size number. Parity claims go out now; accuracy and service-life claims wait for the test report.

What happens next

  • Third-party validation is in progress; results go straight into the claim map.
  • Run same-condition tests against named rivals for accuracy and service life.
  • Add a "hydrogen facilities and energy storage" page to the company site.
  • Keep battery thermal-runaway sensing as an R&D evaluation, not a marketing claim.
  • Refresh the tracker each quarter, within a week of Yole's annual MEMS report.

What this shows

Market intelligence is only useful if people can trust it. Here, the grading rule decided as much as the data did: it kept a 24× market-size range out of the pitch and pointed the launch at claims the company can prove.
