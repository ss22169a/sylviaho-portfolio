Building the Evidence Base for a MEMS Hydrogen Sensor Launch

How a 141-source market tracker narrowed six open questions into three launch decisions, and decided which claims can go out today.

RoleProject Manager
PeriodNov 2026 – present
ToolsGoogle Sheets · datasheets · IEA & industry data · Claude
StatusThird-party validation in progress

The short answer

Hydrogen-sensor market reports disagree by 24.2×, fuel-cell car sales are small and swinging with subsidies, and response speed turned out to be industry parity. So the launch case rests on something else: steady demand at hydrogen facilities, and differentiation that can be proven with data. Third-party validation is in progress; the evidence base makes sure every external claim is sourced and graded before it is used.

3 · How the thinking moved

Diverge → filter → converge → result
  1. Diverge

    Open every question first

    Before collecting a single number, I listed what a buyer, a distributor and an investor would each ask. Six question areas became the tracker's 17 tabs.

  2. Diverge

    Collect wide, from chip to detector

    I mapped vendors at every layer (sensor chips, modules and explosion-proof detectors) because a hydrogen buyer compares across all three, not just against companies like us.

  3. Filter

    Grade every number before using it

    Each figure got an A–D evidence grade and a check date. Only A and B can leave the company. This rule did more to shape the conclusions than any single source.

  4. Filter

    Cross-check, then audit my own work

    Demand indicators were checked against a second independent source wherever one existed. A full audit on Nov 7 found 27 problems, from a mis-keyed year to a rival filed under the wrong principle.

  5. Converge

    Reduce to six conclusions

    Every conclusion lists the sources behind it and the weakest grade among them, so anyone can trace a sentence back to the evidence.

  6. Result

    Turn conclusions into launch decisions

    The output is not a market report. It is three decisions the sales and marketing team can act on now, and a list of what waits for test results.

4 · The decisions

Where to sell firstFacilitiesHydrogen stations, hydrogen production and storage sites, semiconductor fabs and energy storage. Fuel-cell vehicles stay as a showcase, not the volume plan.
How to differentiateProofThermal-conductivity sensing needs no catalyst and no oxygen, so it suits sealed battery rooms and storage sites. The story is built on drift, service life and side-by-side tests.
What to say whenClaim mapNever quote a single market-size number. Parity claims go out now; accuracy and service-life claims wait for the test report.

What happens next

  • Third-party validation is in progress; results go straight into the claim map.
  • Run same-condition tests against named rivals for accuracy and service life.
  • Add a "hydrogen facilities and energy storage" page to the company site.
  • Keep battery thermal-runaway sensing as an R&D evaluation, not a marketing claim.
  • Refresh the tracker each quarter, within a week of Yole's annual MEMS report.

What this shows

Market intelligence is only useful if people can trust it. Here, the grading rule decided as much as the data did: it kept a 24× market-size range out of the pitch and pointed the launch at claims the company can prove.
