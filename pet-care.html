Taking Your Pet to the Vet, but Unable to Describe Its Daily Condition

Finding the gap in daily pet care records through Taiwan pet market data.

ViewPet owners at a vet visit
Period2015–2025
SourcesMOA surveys, TIER estimates, App Store listings · A/B
OutputMarket read, competitor map, product and business plan

Conclusion

Dogs and cats in Taiwan homes grew by about 1.09 million in four years, and regular vet follow-ups are increasingly common, yet most owners still record their pets' daily condition from memory. Large players each stop short of this gap because of their own business models. I am turning it into a pet care app: food, water and physical condition can be logged quickly and handed to the vet at the visit.

1 · Background

Every time I take my pet to the vet, the vet asks: What has it been eating lately? How much? Is it drinking a lot of water? When did things start to seem off? I often can't answer and can only go by memory. Yet these daily details are exactly what a vet needs to judge the condition. I wanted to know whether many owners have the same problem, so I looked at the market data first.

Disputes center on bills above expectations and unclear explanations. Of the 9 public rulings and reports tracked, 8 involve records or communication; the burden of proof usually falls on the owner, and without records the facts are hard to reconstruct.

4 · How I found it

I started from my own experience as a pet owner and used public data to verify the scale. Pets, follow-up visits and medical spending keep rising, yet large players stop at registration, vaccines and purchases in their own stores; the daily record a vet visit needs is still scattered across small apps and not yet brought together.

6 · Entry point: the neutral record layer

01

A neutral food database

Not tied to any one store, it covers common brands across Taiwan; tapping replaces typing, so each meal is logged quickly.

02

A neutral vet clinic database

Chain apps book only their own clinics; this one covers licensed vet clinics across Taiwan, so records can follow the pet on a referral or an emergency visit.

03

Built around the vet visit

Daily records become a vet summary, aligned with the most critical care moment, echoing the high-value touchpoint PetDesk has already proven.

Strategy: launch first for cats and multi-cat homes, where growth is strongest, and make logging a single entry take only seconds as the core experience, to support long-term retention.

7 · What I've done

  • Built a food database of 780+ cat and dog food products.
  • Built a nationwide vet clinic database connected to government open data on licensed clinics by API, with address, opening hours, 24-hour emergency care and price range. Specialties are not yet classified.
  • Planned the app architecture, drew interface wireframes and set up a design system.
  • Developing the iOS version in SwiftUI + Firebase; in progress.

9 · Business model

Taiwan's pet market is estimated at over NT$80 billion, with pet food about 53% of it, and medical care is owners' second-largest expense. This app records exactly those two areas: what a pet eats and how its body is doing.

01

Subscription (first)

Basic logging is free so owners build the habit. Paid features: more pets, trend charts, vet summary export and family sharing. Benchmark: similar apps abroad charge about HK$68 a month or HK$288 a year.

02

Pet food brand partnerships

With anonymized, aggregated data and owner consent, brands can run trials or share ingredient information. Paid placement must never change the database's order or content.

03

Pet insurance partnerships

Pet insurance is small but growing: premiums in the first five months of 2026 rose 8.77% and policies 17.28%. Records could support claims but also be used to flag pre-existing conditions, so data is shared only when the owner chooses.

Go-to-market: start with cat owners in metro areas; partner with vet clinics through in-clinic QR codes and with pet chains (Taiwan has passed 3,500 pet supply stores). Metrics to validate: week-4 retention, share of users who export a summary before a visit, and free-to-paid conversion.

Limitations

  • The 2021, 2023 and 2025 surveys used different sampling (mobile numbers were added from 2023), so part of the growth comes from method changes.
  • Competitor features come from App Store listings and company releases; a feature not mentioned may still exist.
  • Owner and vet interviews have not been done yet. The next step is to interview them and check that these features are really needed.
  • Dispute cases come from public court rulings and news reports, not a random sample; the case tracker keeps growing.
  • The app name is not disclosed yet, and clinic specialties are not yet classified.
